# HARP — 60-Second Walkthrough

## 0–10 seconds: The problem
Open the HARP landing page.

**What to say:**  
“Autonomy can reduce how often humans are needed, but that can make the moments when humans are needed more consequential. HARP explores how an organization could measure whether those humans have current evidence of readiness, not just a training-completion record.”

## 10–30 seconds: The operator
Open **Operations Center** and select a scenario.

**What to show:**  
- assistance request
- available context
- bounded operator actions
- deterministic after-action result
- competency evidence update

**What to say:**  
“The prototype uses scripted decision logic rather than generative AI to decide whether a safety-critical action is correct. A completed scenario becomes evidence tied to specific competencies.”

## 30–45 seconds: Readiness
Click a competency.

**What to show:**  
- performance
- recency
- evidence coverage
- critical errors
- recommendation

**What to say:**  
“HARP separates qualification from readiness. It does not claim a person's actual skill is exactly 64%. The score represents the strength and recency of the evidence that the competency can currently be demonstrated.”

## 45–60 seconds: The organization
Open **Organizational Readiness**.

**What to show:**  
- competency heatmap
- emergency-response coverage
- action/requalification queue
- recommended training block

**What to say:**  
“This is the operational question I wanted HARP to answer: you may have enough qualified people on the schedule, but do you have the right current competencies distributed across the shift to support the mission?”

## Close
“HARP is an operations architecture prototype, not autonomous-driving software. It applies principles from safety-critical qualification, proficiency, readiness reporting, and abnormal-event response to the emerging human layer around Physical AI.”
