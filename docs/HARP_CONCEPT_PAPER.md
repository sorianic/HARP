# HARP: A Human-Readiness Layer for Physical AI

## Abstract
As autonomous systems perform more routine work, human involvement may become less frequent while remaining consequential during uncertainty, abnormal conditions, and emergencies. HARP is an open operational concept exploring how organizations might distinguish formal qualification from the strength and recency of evidence that a human can currently demonstrate required competencies.

## Problem
Traditional learning records are good at answering whether training was completed. Safety-critical operations often need a harder answer: whether the organization has current evidence that the people supporting the operation can perform when needed.

## Concept
HARP models a loop of **Train → Demonstrate → Qualify → Measure → Reinforce**. Scripted scenarios create competency evidence. A transparent readiness engine evaluates performance, recency, coverage, and critical errors. Individual signals then roll into an organizational view that asks whether the shift has sufficient current competency coverage, not merely sufficient headcount.

## Key distinction
HARP does not claim that human skill literally decays to a precise percentage. Its readiness score represents the strength and recency of available evidence of demonstrated proficiency. This preserves a useful distinction between being formally qualified and having strong current evidence of readiness.

## Prototype
v0.1 focuses on fictional Remote Assistance operations for autonomous vehicles. It contains ten synthetic scenarios, eight competencies, an evidence ledger, deterministic evaluation, explainable readiness, a twelve-person synthetic roster, team heatmaps, coverage signals, and training recommendations.

## Broader application
The architecture could be explored in autonomous trucking, robotics, drones, advanced manufacturing, maritime autonomy, and other Physical AI environments where human judgment remains part of the safety architecture.

## Boundary
HARP is a portfolio and research prototype. It is not autonomous-driving software, a vehicle-control system, a regulatory standard, or a validated human-performance or safety model. All operational data and procedures are fictional.
